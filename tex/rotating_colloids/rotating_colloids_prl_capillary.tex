\title{Geometry-Encoded Hidden Orientational Memory}

\author{Vladimir A. Baulin}
\affiliation{Synthetix Institute, Amsterdam, The Netherlands}
\email{vbaulin@synthetix.institute}

\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Memory in disordered soft matter usually requires structural rearrangement or
a persistent external template. We show that elongated colloids caged at a
liquid interface, but free to rotate, instead record preparation history in
their orientations alone. A quadrupolar capillary term registers each pair
against its bond direction, and on a disordered graph these constraints
cannot all be satisfied. Brownian-rotor simulations find strong local pair
and bond-frame order with weak global nematic order: replicas descended from
one prepared state stay correlated for hundreds of rotational-diffusion
times, and a transiently written angular state remains readable after the
field is removed, while finite-size and independent-replica tests show no
equilibrium spin-glass crossing over the tested sizes. A matched loop intervention shows that
global incompatibility creates additional metastable states and changes which
angular patterns form stable memories. Under a maximum-entropy bound, the
field-free state retains at least 0.3 bits of target-orientation information
per particle and is maximized at intermediate positional disorder rather than in
the most disordered limit. Because the same capillary deformation couples to
interfacial undulations, the mechanism also suggests a substrate-free route
to writing, gating, and erasure.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

A material that adapts to its own history must first be able to record one.
The difficulty is that most known records in soft matter are written into
structure: the material must rearrange, or an external template must persist,
and both are hard to revise once the material is in service. A record carried
instead by an internal degree of freedom that remains free to move would let
the same material be written, gated, and erased in place, which is the
functional requirement behind adaptive and reconfigurable matter. In
disordered soft materials, the record may reside in hysteretic elements
activated by cyclic loading, irreversible rearrangements, metastable defects,
or external alignment fields \cite{keim2019}. A sheared suspension
reorganizes until its particles follow reversible trajectories below the
training amplitude \cite{keim2011,paulsen2014}; quenched-disorder nematics
retain a preferred response because fixed local easy axes pin the director
\cite{rotunno2005}. Both leave a record outside the rotational coordinates:
either the configuration has reorganized or a pinning field remains. Slow
relaxation is not sufficient either. Quasi-two-dimensional ellipsoids show
orientational and liquid-glass arrest \cite{zheng2011,roller2021}, but a
slowly relaxing angle need not retain information; a stored state must be
identifiable after the perturbation that prepared it is removed, and must
outlive an otherwise identical control.

The distinction that matters is between relative-angle and bond-frame
coupling. An ordinary nematic interaction constrains \(\theta_i-\theta_j\)
but does not attach a pair orientation to its position, whereas a term
proportional to \(-\cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij})\), with
\(\phi_{ij}\) the centre-to-centre bond angle, registers the pair against
that bond. One such constraint is readily satisfied; in a disordered network
each particle belongs to several differently oriented bonds, and the
constraints cannot all select the same director. The positional graph then
supports a locally correlated angular pattern whose laboratory-frame average
is small. The incompatibility is global: every bond can favour a valid local
orientation, yet following those choices around a closed path need not return
to the starting orientation. No redefinition of the local axes removes this
closed-path mismatch. It divides the angular energy into several locally
stable compromises that can carry different preparation histories.

For ellipsoids at a fluid interface, contact-line deformations generate
precisely this bond-frame interaction. The deformation around each particle
has the far-field form
\(u_i(r,\phi)\propto\Delta u_{\max}(R/r)^2\cos2(\phi-\theta_i)\).
Overlap of two such menisci produces the pair energy
\(U^{\rm cap}_{ij}=-A_4(r_{ij})
\cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij})\), with
\(A_4(r)=3\pi\gamma(\Delta u_{\max})^2(R/r)^4\)
\cite{loudet2005,luo2019}. Here \(\theta_i\) is the apolar orientation,
\(\phi_{ij}=\arg(\bm r_j-\bm r_i)\), \(\gamma\) is the interfacial tension,
\(R\) a particle length scale, and \(\Delta u_{\max}\) the quadrupolar
meniscus amplitude. For \(A_4>0\) an isolated pair minimizes this energy at
\(\theta_i+\theta_j=2\phi_{ij}+m\pi\); around loops of a disordered
multi-neighbour graph these conditions are generally incompatible, forcing a
graph-dependent compromise instead of a common director. Because an
ellipsoidal particle cannot satisfy a uniform contact angle on a flat
interface, the distortion is static, and the resulting pair torque persists
while the particles remain trapped, introducing no one-particle orienting
field.

We consider \(N\) apolar Brownian rotors on a quenched positional graph. The
graph fixes the neighbour and bond directions over the simulated angular
trajectory; it does not impose local orienting axes. The angular energy is
\begin{align}
 U={}&-\sum_{(ij)}J w^-_{ij}\cos2(\theta_i-\theta_j)\nonumber\\
 &-\sum_{(ij)}g w^+_{ij}
 \cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij}).
 \label{eq:model}
\end{align}
Here \(J\) is a first-shell alignment energy and
\(g=A_4(r_0)\) is the capillary energy at the median nearest-neighbour
spacing \(r_0\). We use
\(w^+_{ij}=\min[6,(r_0/r_{ij})^4]\) up to \(2.6a\), where \(a\) is the
undistorted cage spacing, so \(gw^+_{ij}=A_4(r_{ij})\) within the capillary
cutoff; the cap guards against rare near-overlaps and is inactive in every
simulated graph. The
short-range weight is
\(w^-_{ij}=\exp[-\max(r_{ij}/r_0-1,0)/0.2]\) for
\(r_{ij}\leq1.35r_0\) and zero otherwise. Particle centres begin on a
triangular monolayer and receive independent quenched displacements of
standard deviation \(0.16a\), representing a caged interfacial monolayer
without hand-built orientational blocks. The coefficient \(J\) is an
independently calibrated short-range harmonic, not fixed by the far-field
derivation, and \(g\) is the leading pairwise quadrupolar approximation, so
dense-interface many-body corrections must be assessed by comparing
two-particle calibration with collective measurements. Graph construction,
numerical protocols, matched controls, and the run specification are given in
the Supplemental Material.

The \(J\) term favours parallel axes for close neighbours, the \(g\) term
imposes the bond-frame constraint, and varying their ratio tests whether that
constraint retains local angular information without generating a sample-wide
director. The angular density follows the rotational Smoluchowski equation,
integrated in its It\^o Langevin form with \(D_r\Delta t=0.0025\). We
characterize laboratory-frame, relative-pair, and capillary bond-frame order
by
\begin{align}
 S&=\left|N^{-1}\sum_i e^{2i\theta_i}\right|,\nonumber\\
 C_2&=\frac{\sum_{(ij)}w^-_{ij}\cos2(\theta_i-\theta_j)}
 {\sum_{(ij)}w^-_{ij}},\nonumber\\
 G_2&=\frac{\sum_{(ij)}w^+_{ij}
 \cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij})}{\sum_{(ij)}w^+_{ij}}.
 \label{eq:order}
\end{align}
The finite-window statistic
\(q_{\rm EA}=N^{-1}\sum_i|\langle e^{2i\theta_i}\rangle_t|^2\) measures
angular persistence over the sampled trajectory. A quenched graph can give
nonzero local means even in an ergodic state, so \(q_{\rm EA}\) alone cannot
establish glassy nonergodicity. We also separate identical
configurations into two replicas with independent thermal noise and measure
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm split}(t)=N^{-1}\sum_i
 \cos2[\theta_i^{(a)}(t)-\theta_i^{(b)}(t)].
 \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm target}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
 \cos2[\theta_i(t)-\theta_i^{\rm target}],
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
where \(\theta_i^{\rm target}\) is the target configuration.
The fifth map coordinate is
\(C_{\rm window}=\langle\cos2[\theta_i(t_{\rm end})-
\theta_i(t_{\rm start})]\rangle_{i,{\rm replica}}\), the angular
autocorrelation across the sampled window.

These answer separate questions: \(S\) detects a common director, \(C_2\)
parallel neighbours, \(G_2\) satisfaction of the bond-frame torque, and
\(Q_{\rm split}\) how long two copies retain the same state once their
thermal noises are independent. We use \emph{hidden memory} operationally for
low \(S\) with finite local order and long dynamical overlap, and assess its
thermodynamic behaviour separately by finite-size replica tests.

The regime was found and tested in two stages, so that no statistic both
defines and validates it. A dense \((J,g)\) scan was clustered using only the
measured vector, with coupling values and regime names withheld from the fit;
a point inside the resulting population was then tested on new graph sizes
and realizations against \(g=0\), regular-lattice and shuffled-frame
controls, and the long dynamical and equilibrium-overlap calculations were
performed only after that selection.



The taxonomy in Fig.~\ref{fig:map} partitions the responses into four
regimes: a Brownian crossover in the weak-coupling corner, relative alignment
at low \(g\) and large \(J\), capillary-frame memory at large \(g\) and small
\(J\) where \(G_2\) and persistence are large but \(C_2\) is weak, and the
hidden mixed-memory state where both pair harmonics and temporal persistence
coexist while \(S\) stays small. The last contains 169 of the 441 cells, with
centroid \((S,C_2,G_2,q_{\rm EA},C_{\rm window})
=(0.112,0.638,0.414,0.628,0.519)\). The four-cluster solution maximizes the
silhouette score over \(2\leq k\leq6\); all 27 ordered graph-bootstrap
resamples select \(k=4\), with mean adjusted Rand index 0.972 against the
full assignment and at least 0.955 on leaving out any one graph. The hidden
state is therefore a reproducible population of the observable space, not an
imposed threshold or an isolated optimized point.

The marked point lies inside that population and within the targeted
\(1\)--\(5k_{\rm B}T\) scale, and was fixed before the long-time runs. Five
positional-disorder realizations at \(N=1024\) give
\begin{align}
 (S,C_2,G_2,q_{\rm EA})={}&
 (0.0665\!\pm\!0.0038,\,0.627\!\pm\!0.009,\nonumber\\
 &0.447\!\pm\!0.011,\,0.658\!\pm\!0.007).
 \label{eq:selected}
\end{align}
Matched controls distinguish the ingredients. On the same disordered graph
with \(g=0\), \(G_2=0.0000\pm0.0002\) and
\(q_{\rm EA}=0.0225\pm0.0023\); that control also has \emph{larger}
\(S=0.131\pm0.010\) and \(C_2=0.813\pm0.003\) than the capillary system.
The bond-frame term therefore does not add order; it frustrates the alignment
it competes with, suppressing both the director and the neighbour correlation
while creating the persistence. On an undistorted regular graph at the same
couplings the system selects a conventional director, \(S=0.945\). Permuting
the bond angles at fixed weight distribution raises \(q_{\rm EA}\) from 0.658
to 0.748, as expected when geometric compatibility is replaced by stronger
random-bond frustration; that control is no longer a capillary embedding.

Geometry alone drives the crossover. Holding the couplings at
\((J,g)=(4,5)k_{\rm B}T\) and varying only the quenched positional disorder
\(\sigma\) at \(N=576\), the director collapses while the persistence
survives: between \(\sigma=0.02a\) and \(0.28a\), \(S\) falls from
\(0.916\pm0.011\) to \(0.069\pm0.007\), a factor of 13, whereas
\(q_{\rm EA}\) falls only from \(0.815\pm0.040\) to \(0.610\pm0.023\), a
factor of 1.3. Over the same interval \(G_2\) rises from 0.014 to 0.527 while
\(C_2\) falls from 0.927 to 0.630. The bond-angle harmonic of the graph
itself stays below 0.006 throughout, so the ordered state at small \(\sigma\)
is not a residual angular bias of the edge distribution: a nearly regular
graph leaves the bond-frame conditions globally unsatisfiable and follows the
alignment term, and positional disorder is what makes local subsets of them
satisfiable. The equilibrium response saturates, but the retained memory does
not. Write--release--read protocols give a connected retention
\(Q_{\rm target}-S^2\) that falls monotonically above \(\sigma=0.11a\): at
\(N=576\), from \(0.559\pm0.022\) to \(0.472\pm0.030\) at \(0.16a\) and
\(0.386\pm0.026\) at \(0.28a\), the extreme pair differing by 5.1 standard
errors and surviving a restriction to graphs with \(S<0.10\); at \(N=1024\)
the same ordering is better resolved, \(0.514\pm0.011\) against
\(0.454\pm0.010\). Below \(0.11a\) the director is no longer reliably
suppressed: every graph at \(0.08a\) keeps \(S\geq0.161\), and at \(0.05a\)
two of three return to a conventional nematic. The retention maximum therefore
lies near the onset of director suppression,
\(\sigma\simeq0.11a\) (Supplemental Figs.~S3 and S4 and Table~SIV).

The dynamical tests establish memory beyond a static graph-frame
correlation. Split replicas remain near \(Q_{\rm split}=0.5\) over the
long trajectory and end at \(0.462\pm0.010\),
whereas the \(g=0\) control falls below \(0.2\) by \(D_rt=2.7\pm0.3\) and approaches
zero. Following a quench, the two-time correlation at lag
\(D_r\Delta t=30\) increases from \(0.676\) for \(D_rt_w=62.5\) to \(0.701\)
for \(D_rt_w=375\). Because the graph-to-graph scatter is a common offset,
the increment is resolved graph by graph: it is
\(+0.0253\pm0.0019\) and positive for every realization, against a
between-graph spread of 0.006 in the correlation itself. This waiting-time
dependence is consistent with finite-size aging. In the write-release-read protocol, a
temporary local conjugate field produces a target overlap of \(0.863\pm0.009\); after
the field is removed, the overlap remains \(0.441\pm0.021\) at \(D_rt=625\), while
the state remains weakly nematic with \(S=0.065\pm0.008\). Without capillary
coupling, the same write protocol decays below \(0.2\) only
\(D_r(t-t_{\rm off})=4.3\pm0.3\) after field removal and
ends at zero. The capillary system therefore preserves a record for 625
rotational times, more than two orders of magnitude beyond the matched
control. The interfacial deformation itself relaxes far faster:
\(\tau_{\rm int}\sim\eta\ell/\gamma\lesssim10^{-5}\,\mathrm s\) against
experimental \(D_r^{-1}\gtrsim10^{-2}\,\mathrm s\) (Supplemental Material),
so the retained overlap is an angular state of the particles, not a slowly
relaxing deformation of the host interface.

Figure~\ref{fig:activated} resolves how retained overlap changes with
coupling, over eight values of \(\lambda\) with five disordered graphs each.
The split-replica overlap retained at \(D_rT_{\rm obs}=625\) rises
monotonically from \(-0.012\pm0.006\) at \(\lambda=0.45\) to
\(0.041\pm0.010\) at \(0.60\), \(0.405\pm0.008\) at \(0.90\) and
\(0.691\pm0.005\) at \(1.40\); the written-state values rise in parallel,
\(0.007\pm0.009\) to \(0.087\pm0.014\), \(0.466\pm0.016\) and
\(0.497\pm0.010\). The matched \(g=0\) control is consistent with zero at
every coupling, reaching \(0.003\pm0.019\) at \(\lambda=1.40\), so the two
branches separate by \(3.4\sigma\) at \(\lambda=0.60\) and by \(35\)--\(45\)
\(\sigma\) for \(\lambda\ge0.90\). Retention is therefore reported as a
separation from a null control rather than as a ratio, which is unstable
against a denominator consistent with zero.
Capillary geometry therefore opens a long-lived dynamical retention regime
rather than only raising a static correlator. Panel (c) shows why a
fixed-window \(q_{\rm EA}\) is insufficient alone: at \(\lambda=0.30\) it
decays to zero as the read window grows, whereas at \(1.40\) it holds near
0.78 at the longest window.

Finite-size scaling identifies what is stored. Between \(N=144\) and 2304 the
director falls from \(S=0.151\) to 0.040 as \(N^{-0.479}\), and independently
equilibrated replicas obey \(\langle|Q_{ab}|\rangle\propto N^{-0.466}\),
whereas \(C_2\simeq0.63\), \(G_2\simeq0.45\) and \(q_{\rm EA}\simeq0.65\)
stay of order unity, the fitted \(q_{\rm EA}\) exponent being 0.011. The
overlap susceptibility saturates and \(\xi_L/L\) has no common crossing
(Supplemental Figs.~S5 and S6). Independent preparations therefore select
different angular compromises while descendants of one preparation inherit
the same local basin, and this history-conditioned overlap is the memory
variable.

The closed paths identify the origin of the basin multiplicity. To isolate
this mechanism, we use a strong-locking reduction in which a locally ordered
region has two apolar choices \(s_b=\pm1\), with
\(H_{\rm eff}=-\sum_{bc}K_{bc}s_bs_c\). The loop product
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal W_\gamma=\prod_{(bc)\in\gamma}{\rm sgn}(K_{bc})
 \label{eq:loop-holonomy}
\end{equation}
is unchanged if any local axis is reversed. A loop with
\(\mathcal W_\gamma=-1\) cannot satisfy all of its pair preferences at once.
We removed these loop mismatches while preserving the graph and every
\(|K_{bc}|\). Across 24 independently disordered graphs, the original
networks contained \(5.58\) more one-flip-stable states on average, with a
graph-bootstrap 95\% interval of \(2.67\)--\(8.83\). In the corresponding
continuous-angle model, the average retention across four prescribed patterns
included zero, while individual patterns changed in opposite directions.
Loop incompatibility therefore creates the alternatives among which the
material can choose; it does not lengthen every memory. Preparation selects
one compromise, and the collective barriers set its lifetime. The
Supplemental Material gives the intervention, gauge control, and
target-resolved dynamics.

The spatial and temporal ranges separate sharply. Real-space analysis gives
first-shell connected \(C_2(r)=0.557\) and bond-frame \(G_2(r)=0.505\), both
reaching the noise scale by \(r/a\simeq1.73\) (Supplemental Fig.~S2), and the
overlap correlation length from the second moment of \(\chi_Q(\bm k)\) agrees
independently, never exceeding \(1.49a\) at any size or coupling. Yet the
same state retains \(Q_{\rm split}=0.462\) at \(D_rt=625\). Correlations in
space end within two particle spacings while descendants of one preparation
stay correlated for hundreds of rotational times: the graph stores history
through local compatibility constraints, not a concealed bulk director.

The model assumes a quenched neighbour graph, so a realization needs the
graph to persist during angular relaxation:
\(\tau_{\rm int}\ll D_r^{-1}\ll\tau_{\rm cage}\), where \(\tau_{\rm int}\) is
the interfacial-deformation time and \(\tau_{\rm cage}\) the neighbour-network
lifetime. The first inequality makes the capillary torque quasistatic, the
second lets the orientations explore the landscape before the graph
rearranges, and the Supplemental Material estimates \(\tau_{\rm int}D_r\lesssim10^{-3}\).
Caged translation with nearly free rotation is realized for charged silica
rods \cite{besseling2026}, and DNA contacts give Brownian colloidal pivots
\cite{melio2026}. The candidate here is a dense monolayer of micrometre-scale
polymer ellipsoids at an air--water interface, where adsorption confines the
particles to a plane and electrostatic repulsion, capillary attraction and
crowding produce a long-lived cage. The scales are accessible: at
\(\gamma=10\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}\) and \(r/R=3\), \(A_4=1\)--\(5k_{\rm B}T\)
needs \(\Delta u_{\max}=1.9\)--\(4.2\,\mathrm{nm}\). Smooth ellipsoids
interact far more strongly \cite{loudet2005}, while roughness, porosity,
wetting and ionic strength reduce the attraction
\cite{trevenen2023,rahman2026}; excessive porosity removes the quadrupole, so
the target is an intermediate interaction, not the weakest capillarity.

Video microscopy gives \(\bm r_i(t)\) and \(\theta_i(t)\), and the
persistent-neighbour graph fixes \(\phi_{ij}\), \(S\), \(C_2\), \(G_2\) and
the two-time overlap with no fitted hidden variables. Calibrating the pair
torque on two particles first, from \(p(\theta_i,\theta_j\mid r)\),
determines \(J\) and \(g\) independently of the collective data and tests the
\(r^{-4}\) decay (Supplemental Material). The minimal memory test then needs
no writing apparatus: perturb the layer, remove the perturbation, and compare
the angular autocorrelation against a low-quadrupole control. Polarized
holographic traps \cite{lapointe2011} would supply temporary torques for a
write--release--read test, reading out only after removal.

The water surface is not a passive substrate for this memory. The same
quadrupolar deformation that stores the state also couples the state to
undulations of the host interface, which gives the layer a mechanically
native control channel that no solid-supported realization has, and the three
couplings act on different terms of the model. A transient deviatoric
curvature \(\Delta c\) supplies the one-body torque
\(U_i^{\rm curv}=-\pi\gamma H_pR_p^2\Delta c(\bm r_i,t)\cos2(\theta_i-\alpha_i)\),
with \(H_p\), \(R_p\) the amplitude and lateral scale of the particle
quadrupole and \(\alpha_i\) the principal-curvature axis; shaped interfaces
already orient interfacial rods \cite{cavallaro2011}, so a curvature pulse
writes and flattening removes the torque before readout. A uniform capillary
wave carries no spatial code but modulates the pair term, since
\(g\propto\gamma(\Delta u)^2\) and
\(\Delta u(t)=\Delta u_0[1+\epsilon\cos\omega t]\) oscillate the barriers at
\(\omega\) and \(2\omega\), gating annealing or erasure. A wave that
displaces particles instead changes \(\sigma\), and because retention peaks
near \(0.11a\) the sign of that response is predicted rather than free:
loosening a layer past the peak erases it, annealing one toward the peak
consolidates it. Capillary-wave annealing and ultrasound-driven capillary
rearrangement are both established \cite{caso2025,huerre2018}, and tracking
\(\bm r_i(t)\) with \(\theta_i(t)\) separates the channels, since only the
last moves the graph.

The demonstrated function is preparation-dependent, particle-resolved angular
retention without a common director, read out directly by video or
polarization microscopy; rewriting and local mechanical readout are proposed
tests rather than simulation results. The retained overlap bounds the
target-orientation information: treating the residual angular error as the maximum-entropy distribution at
the measured \(\langle\cos2\Delta\theta\rangle\) bounds it below by
\(0.296\pm0.029\) bits per rotor after \(D_rt=625\), so the sum of the
particlewise bounds for an \(N=1024\) layer is \(303\pm30\) bits, a fifth of
the \(1.481\pm0.051\) bits per rotor present under the
field, against \(7\times10^{-5}\) for the \(g=0\) control. Endurance and
spatial addressability remain to be measured.

The Supplemental Material gives a programmable counterpart in which a mosaic
of patterned easy axes supplies the local directions instead of the bond
geometry. There, neighbour coupling amplifies a weak surface cue by a factor
of 8.5 without selecting a common director, and raises the post-removal
overlap half-life thirtyfold (Supplemental Figs.~S7 and S8). Such patterns
are fabricable by digital photoalignment \cite{wu2012}, but the code is
externally specified; the pair model tests memory encoded autonomously.

Positional disorder prevents global director selection while capillary
quadrupoles convert every bond direction into a local condition. Their
closed-path incompatibility creates several metastable angular compromises;
preparation selects one of them, and interactions hinder escape from it.
Descendants of one preparation consequently remain correlated hundreds of
rotational times after spatial correlations have ended. Distinct local
angular histories can coexist without a common optical axis.

Read as a design principle, this is a memory a material could develop rather
than be given. Nothing here is addressed, patterned, or externally templated:
the code is the bond geometry the layer already has, the carrier stays free
to move, and each preparation selects its own compromise among the same fixed
constraints, so a layer records how it was made and driven while remaining,
to a polarizer, unchanged. The disorder dependence sets the operating point,
and it is not the naive limit: retention is largest near the onset of
director suppression, so an adaptive layer would be tuned toward its ordering
crossover rather than away from it. Whether a layer can
be driven there by its own environment, with interfacial undulations both
gating the barriers and adjusting the geometry that sets them, is the
question this mechanism raises.
