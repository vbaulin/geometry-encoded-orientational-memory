\title{Geometry-Encoded Hidden Orientational Memory}

\author{Vladimir A. Baulin}
\affiliation{Synthetix Institute, Amsterdam, The Netherlands}
\email{info@synthetix.institute}

\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Memory in disordered soft matter usually requires structural rearrangement or
a persistent external template. We show that elongated colloids caged at a
liquid interface, but free to rotate, instead record preparation history in
their orientations alone. A quadrupolar capillary term registers each pair
against its bond direction, and on a disordered graph these constraints
cannot all be satisfied. Brownian-rotor simulations find strong local pair
and bond-frame order with weak global nematic order: replicas descended from
one prepared state stay correlated for hundreds of rotational-diffusion
times, and a transiently written angular state remains readable after the
field is removed, while finite-size and independent-replica tests exclude an
equilibrium spin-glass crossing. The state holds at least 0.3 bits per
particle, is maximized at intermediate positional disorder rather than in the
most disordered limit, and couples to interfacial undulations that write,
gate, and erase it without a patterned substrate.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

A material that adapts to its own history must first be able to record one.
The difficulty is that most known records in soft matter are written into
structure: the material must rearrange, or an external template must persist,
and both are hard to revise once the material is in service. A record carried
instead by an internal degree of freedom that remains free to move would let
the same material be written, gated, and erased in place, which is the
functional requirement behind adaptive and reconfigurable matter. In
disordered soft materials, the microscopic record
may reside in hysteretic elements activated by cyclic loading, irreversible
particle rearrangements, metastable defects, external alignment fields, or
persistent orientational order \cite{keim2019}. A suspension repeatedly
sheared to a fixed strain amplitude gradually reorganizes until
its particles follow reversible trajectories below that amplitude. If the
strain is later increased, irreversible motion resumes near the previously
applied value, revealing the training history
\cite{keim2011,paulsen2014}. In this form of memory, particle rearrangements
leave the physical record of the earlier strain.

Quenched-disorder nematics use a different carrier. Fixed local easy axes
pin the director orientation, such that the material retains a preferred orientational response after the applied field is changed \cite{rotunno2005}. Part of the
stored information resides in a permanent external environment rather than
in the freely moving colloids. Both mechanisms therefore leave a
persistent record outside the rotational coordinates themselves: either the
colloid configuration has reorganized or a local pinning field remains.

Anisotropic colloids show why slow rotation must be distinguished from
memory. Quasi-two-dimensional ellipsoids exhibit orientational and
liquid-glass arrest \cite{zheng2011,roller2021}, but a slowly relaxing angle
need not retain information about an earlier state. A stored angular state
must remain identifiable after the perturbation that prepared it has been
removed, and it must outlive an otherwise identical control. We ask whether
pair interactions can provide this record while particle positions remain
unchanged and no local orienting field remains.

The relevant distinction is between relative-angle and bond-frame coupling.
An ordinary nematic interaction constrains
\(\theta_i-\theta_j\), but does not attach a pair orientation to its position.
A term proportional to
\(-\cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij})\), where \(\phi_{ij}\) is the
centre-to-centre bond angle, instead registers the pair against that bond.
One such constraint is readily satisfied. In a disordered network, however,
each particle belongs to several differently oriented bonds, and the
corresponding constraints cannot all select the same director. The
positional graph can then support a locally correlated angular pattern whose
laboratory-frame average is small.

Brownian-rotor simulations expose four connected signatures of this state.
A 441-cell scan resolves a finite region with weak global order, finite local
pair order, and long angular persistence; the population survives exact
resampling of the three positional graphs. Increasing the system from
\(N=144\) to 2304 removes the global director but leaves the local
correlators unchanged. Replicas separated from one prepared configuration
remain related for hundreds of rotational times, whereas independently
equilibrated replicas lose their mutual overlap as \(N^{-1/2}\). Finally,
the spatial correlations themselves vanish within approximately two particle
spacings. The memory is therefore carried by the history of local angular
neighbourhoods, rather than by a hidden sample-wide orientation.

For ellipsoids at a fluid interface, contact-line deformations generate
precisely this bond-frame interaction. The deformation around each particle
has the far-field form
\(u_i(r,\phi)\propto\Delta u_{\max}(R/r)^2\cos2(\phi-\theta_i)\).
Overlap of two such menisci produces the pair energy
\(U^{\rm cap}_{ij}=-A_4(r_{ij})
\cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij})\), with
\(A_4(r)=3\pi\gamma(\Delta u_{\max})^2(R/r)^4\)
\cite{loudet2005,luo2019}. For \(A_4>0\), an isolated pair minimizes this
energy when
\(\theta_i+\theta_j=2\phi_{ij}+m\pi\), with integer \(m\).
On a disordered multi-neighbour graph, the corresponding conditions around
loops are generally incompatible, forcing a graph-dependent angular
compromise instead of a common director. Here \(\theta_i\) is the apolar particle
orientation, \(\phi_{ij}=\arg(\bm r_j-\bm r_i)\) is the bond angle,
\(\gamma\) is the interfacial tension, \(R\) is a particle length scale, and
\(\Delta u_{\max}\) is the quadrupolar meniscus amplitude. Because an
ellipsoidal particle cannot satisfy a uniform contact angle on a perfectly
flat interface, the interface adopts a static distortion fixed by the contact-angle
boundary condition. The resulting pair torque persists while the particles
remain trapped at the interface and introduces no one-particle orienting
field.

We consider \(N\) apolar Brownian rotors on a quenched positional graph. The
graph fixes the neighbour and bond directions over the simulated angular
trajectory; it does not impose local orienting axes. The angular energy is
\begin{align}
 U={}&-\sum_{(ij)}J w^-_{ij}\cos2(\theta_i-\theta_j)\nonumber\\
 &-\sum_{(ij)}g w^+_{ij}
 \cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij}).
 \label{eq:model}
\end{align}
Here \(J\) is a first-shell alignment energy and
\(g=A_4(r_0)\) is the capillary energy at the median nearest-neighbour
spacing \(r_0\). We use
\(w^+_{ij}=\min[6,(r_0/r_{ij})^4]\) up to \(2.6a\), where \(a\) is the
undistorted cage spacing, so \(gw^+_{ij}=A_4(r_{ij})\) within the capillary
cutoff; the cap guards against rare near-overlaps and is inactive in every
simulated graph. The
short-range weight is
\(w^-_{ij}=\exp[-\max(r_{ij}/r_0-1,0)/0.2]\) for
\(r_{ij}\leq1.35r_0\) and zero otherwise. Particle centres begin on a
triangular monolayer and receive independent quenched displacements of
standard deviation \(0.16a\). This construction represents a caged
interfacial monolayer while avoiding hand-built orientational blocks.
The coefficient \(J\) is an independently calibrated effective short-range
harmonic; it is not fixed by the far-field capillary derivation. The \(g\)
term is the leading pairwise quadrupolar approximation. Dense-interface
many-body corrections must therefore be assessed by comparing two-particle
calibration with collective measurements.
Graph construction, numerical protocols, matched controls, and the
publication-scale run specification are given in the Supplemental Material.

The \(J\) term favours parallel axes for close neighbours, whereas the \(g\)
term implements the bond-frame constraint described above. Varying their
relative strength tests whether that constraint can retain local angular
information without generating a sample-wide director.

The angular probability density
\(P(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_N,t)\) follows the rotational Smoluchowski
equation
\begin{equation}
 \partial_tP=D_r\sum_i\partial_{\theta_i}
 \left(\partial_{\theta_i}P+
 P\,\partial_{\theta_i}\frac{U}{k_{\rm B}T}\right).
 \label{eq:smol}
\end{equation}
The simulations integrate its It\^o Langevin form with
\(D_r\Delta t=0.0025\). We characterize laboratory-frame order, relative
pair order, and capillary bond-frame order by
\begin{align}
 S&=\left|N^{-1}\sum_i e^{2i\theta_i}\right|,\nonumber\\
 C_2&=\frac{\sum_{(ij)}w^-_{ij}\cos2(\theta_i-\theta_j)}
 {\sum_{(ij)}w^-_{ij}},\nonumber\\
 G_2&=\frac{\sum_{(ij)}w^+_{ij}
 \cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij})}{\sum_{(ij)}w^+_{ij}}.
 \label{eq:order}
\end{align}
The finite-window statistic
\(q_{\rm EA}=N^{-1}\sum_i|\langle e^{2i\theta_i}\rangle_t|^2\) measures
angular persistence over the sampled trajectory. A quenched graph can give
nonzero local means even in an ergodic state, so \(q_{\rm EA}\) alone cannot
establish glassy nonergodicity. We also separate identical
configurations into two replicas with independent thermal noise and measure
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm split}(t)=N^{-1}\sum_i
 \cos2[\theta_i^{(a)}(t)-\theta_i^{(b)}(t)].
 \label{eq:split}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm target}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
 \cos2[\theta_i(t)-\theta_i^{\rm target}],
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
where \(\theta_i^{\rm target}\) is the target configuration.
The fifth map coordinate is
\(C_{\rm window}=\langle\cos2[\theta_i(t_{\rm end})-
\theta_i(t_{\rm start})]\rangle_{i,{\rm replica}}\), the angular
autocorrelation across the sampled window.

These observables answer separate physical questions. The magnitude \(S\)
detects a common director; \(C_2\) detects parallel neighbouring particles;
\(G_2\) measures satisfaction of the capillary bond-frame torque; and
\(Q_{\rm split}\) asks how long two copies retain the same angular state after
their thermal noises become independent. We use \emph{hidden memory} as an
operational description for low \(S\) accompanied by finite local order and
long dynamical overlap. Its thermodynamic behavior is assessed independently
by finite-size replica tests.

The regime was found and tested in two stages. First, a dense \((J,g)\) scan
was clustered using only the measured vector
\((S,C_2,G_2,q_{\rm EA},C_{\rm window})\); the coupling values and regime names
were withheld from the fit. A point inside the resulting mixed-memory
population was then tested on new graph sizes and positional realizations,
against \(g=0\), regular-lattice, and shuffled-frame controls. Long
split-replica, write--release--read, aging, and equilibrium-overlap
calculations were performed after this selection. Thus the map identifies
the candidate population, whereas the independent dynamical and finite-size
tests determine what that population means.



The finite-size taxonomy in Fig.~\ref{fig:map} partitions the measured
responses into four regimes. A Brownian crossover occupies
the weak-coupling corner. Relative alignment dominates at low \(g\) and
large \(J\). Capillary-frame memory occupies large \(g\) and small \(J\),
where \(G_2\) and persistence are large but \(C_2\) is weak or negative. The fourth
region is the hidden mixed-memory state, where both pair harmonics and
temporal persistence coexist while \(S\) remains small. It contains 169 of
the 441 cells and has centroid
\((S,C_2,G_2,q_{\rm EA},C_{\rm window})
=(0.112,0.638,0.414,0.628,0.519)\).
Within the tested observable representation, the four-cluster solution maximizes the silhouette score over
\(2\leq k\leq6\). Exact enumeration of the 27 ordered graph-bootstrap
resamples of the three sampled graphs selects \(k=4\) in every case, with mean adjusted Rand index
0.972 relative to the full assignment; leaving out any one graph gives
adjusted Rand index at least 0.955. Thus the hidden state is a reproducible
population of the measured observable space rather than an imposed threshold
or an isolated optimized point.

The marked point lies inside the mixed-memory population and within
the experimentally targeted \(1\)--\(5k_{\rm B}T\) interaction scale. It was
fixed before the long-time validation runs. Five positional-disorder
realizations at \(N=1024\) give
\begin{align}
 (S,C_2,G_2,q_{\rm EA})={}&
 (0.0665\!\pm\!0.0038,\,0.627\!\pm\!0.009,\nonumber\\
 &0.447\!\pm\!0.011,\,0.658\!\pm\!0.007).
 \label{eq:selected}
\end{align}
Matched controls distinguish the ingredients. On the same disordered graph
with \(g=0\), \(G_2=0.0000\pm0.0002\) and
\(q_{\rm EA}=0.0225\pm0.0023\); that control also has \emph{larger}
\(S=0.131\pm0.010\) and \(C_2=0.813\pm0.003\) than the capillary system.
The bond-frame term therefore does not add order. It frustrates the
alignment it competes with, suppressing both the common director and the
neighbour correlation while creating the persistence. On an undistorted regular graph at the
same \(J\) and \(g\), the system selects a conventional global director,
with \(S=0.945\). Randomly permuting the bond angles while preserving the
weight distribution increases \(q_{\rm EA}\) from 0.658 to 0.748, as expected
when geometric compatibility is replaced by stronger random-bond frustration;
this control no longer represents a capillary embedding.

Geometry alone drives the crossover. Holding the couplings at
\((J,g)=(4,5)k_{\rm B}T\) and varying only the quenched positional disorder
\(\sigma\) at \(N=576\), the director collapses while the persistence
survives: between \(\sigma=0.02a\) and \(0.28a\), \(S\) falls from
\(0.916\pm0.011\) to \(0.069\pm0.007\), a factor of 13, whereas
\(q_{\rm EA}\) falls only from \(0.815\pm0.040\) to \(0.610\pm0.023\), a
factor of 1.3. Over the same interval \(G_2\) rises from 0.014 to 0.527 while
\(C_2\) falls from 0.927 to 0.630. The bond-angle harmonic of the graph
itself stays below 0.006 throughout, so the ordered state at small \(\sigma\)
is not a residual angular bias of the edge distribution: a nearly regular
graph leaves the bond-frame conditions globally unsatisfiable and follows the
alignment term, and positional disorder is what makes local subsets of them
satisfiable. The equilibrium response saturates, but the retained memory does
not. Write--release--read protocols at \(N=576\) give a connected retention
\(Q_{\rm target}-S^2\) that falls monotonically above \(\sigma=0.11a\), from
\(0.559\pm0.022\) to \(0.472\pm0.030\) at \(0.16a\) and \(0.386\pm0.026\) at
\(0.28a\), the extreme pair differing by 5.1 standard errors and surviving a
restriction to graphs with \(S<0.10\). Below \(0.11a\) the director is no
longer reliably suppressed: every graph at \(0.08a\) keeps \(S\geq0.161\),
and at \(0.05a\) two of three return to a conventional nematic. The geometry
that stores the most is therefore the least disorder that still hides the
director, \(\sigma\simeq0.11a\) (Supplemental Fig.~S3 and
Table~SIV).

The dynamical tests establish memory beyond a static graph-frame
correlation. Split replicas remain near \(Q_{\rm split}=0.5\) over the
long trajectory and end at \(0.462\pm0.010\),
whereas the \(g=0\) control falls below \(0.2\) by \(D_rt=2.7\pm0.3\) and approaches
zero. Following a quench, the two-time correlation at lag
\(D_r\Delta t=30\) increases from \(0.676\) for \(D_rt_w=62.5\) to \(0.701\)
for \(D_rt_w=375\). Because the graph-to-graph scatter is a common offset,
the increment is resolved graph by graph: it is
\(+0.0253\pm0.0019\) and positive for every realization, against a
between-graph spread of 0.006 in the correlation itself. This waiting-time
dependence is consistent with finite-size aging. In the write-release-read protocol, a
temporary local conjugate field produces a target overlap of \(0.863\pm0.009\); after
the field is removed, the overlap remains \(0.441\pm0.021\) at \(D_rt=625\), while
the state remains weakly nematic with \(S=0.065\pm0.008\). Without capillary
coupling, the same write protocol decays below \(0.2\) only
\(D_r(t-t_{\rm off})=4.3\pm0.3\) after field removal and
ends at zero. The capillary system therefore preserves a measurable record
for at least 625 rotational times, more than two orders of magnitude beyond
the decorrelation time of the matched control. We estimate
\(\tau_{\rm int}\sim\eta_{\rm eff}\ell/\gamma\) for interfacial deformation relaxation (Supplemental Material), where \(\eta_{\rm eff}\) is the effective interfacial viscosity and \(\ell\) the lateral extent of the meniscus mode;
with \(\tau_{\rm int}\lesssim10^{-5}\,\mathrm s\) and experimental \(D_r^{-1}\gtrsim10^{-2}\,\mathrm s\),
\(\tau_{\rm int}D_r\ll1\). The retained overlap is therefore an angular
state of the interacting particles, not a slowly relaxing deformation of
the host interface.

coupling. We scanned eight
values of \(\lambda\), with five independently disordered graphs at each

\(10.8\pm0.5\) at \(\lambda=0.45\) to \(79.1\pm3.5\) at \(\lambda=0.60\),
and reaches \(307.0\pm5.6\) at \(\lambda=0.90\). At the latter coupling the

\(\lambda=0.90\); the matched \(g=0\) value is \(7.16\pm0.59\), giving a

Panel (c)
also shows why a fixed-window \(q_{\rm EA}\) is insufficient by itself. At
\(\lambda=0.30\) it decays to zero as the read window grows, whereas at

Finite-size scaling identifies what is stored. Between \(N=144\) and 2304,
the global director decreases from \(S=0.151\) to 0.040, with
\(S\propto N^{-0.479}\). Independently equilibrated replicas similarly obey
\(\langle|Q_{ab}|\rangle\propto N^{-0.466}\). By contrast,
\(C_2\simeq0.63\), \(G_2\simeq0.45\), and finite-window
\(q_{\rm EA}\simeq0.65\) remain of order unity; the fitted exponent of
\(q_{\rm EA}\) is 0.011. The overlap susceptibility saturates and
\(\xi_L/L\) has no common crossing (Supplemental Figs.~S4 and S5).
Independent preparations therefore select different angular compromises,
whereas descendants of one preparation inherit the same local basin. This
history-conditioned overlap is the memory variable.

The spatial and temporal ranges separate sharply. A real-space analysis in
Supplemental Fig.~S2 gives first-shell connected \(C_2(r)=0.557\) and
bond-frame \(G_2(r)=0.505\); both reach the operational noise scale by
\(r/a\simeq1.73\). The overlap correlation length obtained independently
from the second moment of \(\chi_Q(\bm k)\) agrees: averaged over graphs it
never exceeds \(1.49a\) at any size or coupling on the scanned ray. The same state retains
\(Q_{\rm split}=0.462\) at \(D_rt=625\). Spatial orientational correlations
therefore disappear within approximately two particle spacings, yet systems
descended from the same prepared state remain correlated for hundreds of
rotational-diffusion times. The positional graph stores history through
local compatibility constraints rather than through a concealed bulk
director.

The simulation assumes a quenched neighbour graph throughout each angular
trajectory. A realization without permanent positional pinning therefore
requires the neighbour graph to persist during angular relaxation. Experiments on
charged silica rods have realized caged translation with nearly free rotation
\cite{besseling2026}, and DNA-based contacts have produced Brownian colloidal
pivots \cite{melio2026}. For the capillary interaction studied here, a
candidate realization is a dense monolayer of micrometre-scale polymer
ellipsoids at an air--water or oil--water interface. Interfacial adsorption
confines the particles to a plane, while electrostatic repulsion, capillary
attraction, and crowding can produce a long-lived lateral cage. Surface
roughness or partial porosity provides a means to reduce the capillary
attraction while retaining the quadrupolar contact-line deformation
\cite{trevenen2023,rahman2026}.
Stability of the effective rotor model requires the hierarchy
\begin{equation}
 \tau_{\rm int}\ll D_r^{-1}\ll\tau_{\rm cage},
 \label{eq:cage-condition}
\end{equation}
where \(\tau_{\rm int}\) is the relaxation time of the interfacial
deformation, \(D_r^{-1}\) is the rotational diffusion time, and
\(\tau_{\rm cage}\) is the lifetime of the neighbour network. The first
inequality makes the capillary torque quasistatic; the second allows the
orientations to explore the memory landscape before the positional graph
rearranges. The Supplemental Material gives the estimate of
\(\tau_{\rm int}D_r\).

The physical scales are accessible. For
\(\gamma=10\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}\) and \(r/R=3\), the far-field amplitude
gives \(A_4=1\)--\(5k_{\rm B}T\) for
\(\Delta u_{\max}=1.9\)--\(4.2\,\mathrm{nm}\). Smooth micron-scale
ellipsoids can interact much more strongly than \(k_{\rm B}T\)
\cite{loudet2005}; surface roughness, porosity, wetting angle, ionic strength,
and particle separation provide routes to reduce or balance the attraction
against electrostatic repulsion \cite{trevenen2023,rahman2026}. Excessive
porosity removes the quadrupolar deformation, so the target is an intermediate
interaction range, not the weakest possible capillarity.

An experiment would adsorb optically resolvable ellipsoids at a flat
fluid--fluid interface and form a dense repulsive monolayer whose neighbour
network persists over many rotational diffusion times. Video microscopy
gives \(\bm r_i(t)\) and \(\theta_i(t)\); the persistent-neighbour graph then
determines \(\phi_{ij}\), \(S\), \(C_2\), \(G_2\), and the two-time overlap
without fitted hidden variables. The pair interaction should be calibrated
before the many-particle test. Two particles held at a controlled separation
give the conditional histogram
\(p(\theta_i,\theta_j\mid r)\), from which
\(U_{\rm eff}/k_{\rm B}T=-\ln p+\mathrm{const.}\) is obtained. Fitting its two
second harmonics measures \(Jw^-_{ij}\) and \(gw^+_{ij}\); repeating the
measurement at several separations tests the predicted \(r^{-4}\) capillary
decay. The many-particle calculation can then be compared with experiment
without fitting an orientational torque to the collective data.

The minimal memory test requires no writing apparatus: perturb the layer,
remove the perturbation, and measure the particle-resolved angular
autocorrelation against a low-quadrupole control. A write--release--read test
can use an array of independently polarized holographic traps to apply
temporary particle-resolved torques; dynamic holographic tweezers have
demonstrated orientational control of complex colloids \cite{lapointe2011}.
Readout begins only after the applied field has been removed.

The water surface is not a passive substrate for this memory. The same
quadrupolar deformation that stores the state also couples the state to
undulations of the host interface, which gives the layer a mechanically
native control channel that no solid-supported realization has. Three
couplings follow, and they act on different terms of the model. First, a
transient deviatoric curvature \(\Delta c(\bm r,t)\) supplies a one-body
torque,
\[
 U_i^{\rm curv}=-\pi\gamma H_pR_p^2\Delta c(\bm r_i,t)
 \cos2(\theta_i-\alpha_i),
\]
with \(H_p\) and \(R_p\) the amplitude and lateral scale of the
particle-induced quadrupole and \(\alpha_i\) the local principal-curvature
axis. Shaped interfaces already orient and steer interfacial rods
\cite{cavallaro2011}, so a curvature pulse writes a pattern and flattening
the interface removes the torque before readout. Second, a nearly uniform
capillary wave carries no spatial code but modulates the pair term itself:
with \(g\propto\gamma(\Delta u)^2\) and
\(\Delta u(t)=\Delta u_0[1+\epsilon\cos\omega t]\), the barriers oscillate at
\(\omega\) and \(2\omega\), gating annealing or erasure. Third, and now
quantitatively, a wave that displaces the particles changes the positional
disorder \(\sigma\) itself. Because retention varies non-monotonically with
\(\sigma\) and peaks near \(0.11a\), the sign of the response is predicted
rather than free: undulations that loosen a layer already past the peak erase
it, whereas undulations that anneal a layer toward the peak consolidate it.
Capillary-wave annealing of air--water colloidal monolayers and
ultrasound-driven capillary rearrangement have both been observed
\cite{caso2025,huerre2018}. Tracking \(\bm r_i(t)\) with \(\theta_i(t)\)
separates the three channels, since only the third moves the graph.

The demonstrated function is preparation-dependent, particle-resolved
angular retention without a common director. Video or polarization
microscopy provides the direct readout through angular overlap. Rewriting by
a temporary field and local mechanical readout are experimental tests rather
than results of the autonomous pair simulations. Capillary microstructure is
known to control the mechanics of particle layers \cite{luo2019,rahman2026},
which makes local modulus a candidate secondary readout. The retained
overlap sets a capacity. Treating the residual angular error as the
maximum-entropy distribution at the measured
\(\langle\cos2\Delta\theta\rangle\) gives a lower bound of
\(0.296\pm0.029\) bits per rotor after \(D_rt=625\), so a single
\(N=1024\) layer still holds \(303\pm30\) bits, one fifth of the
\(1.481\pm0.051\) bits per rotor present while the field is applied. The
matched \(g=0\) control retains \(7\times10^{-5}\) bits per rotor. Device
endurance and spatial addressability remain to be measured.

The Supplemental Material gives a second, experimentally programmable
construction. A mosaic of shallow grooves or patterned easy axes prescribes
a local direction \(\alpha_i\), while neighbour coupling amplifies and retains
that pattern. The simulated device also weakens coupling across patch
boundaries. In terms of the dimensionless surface and neighbour rates
\(h\) and \(j\), at \(h=0.2\), increasing \(j\) from 0 to 3 raises the
registration from \(G_2=0.0965\) to 0.824 without producing a common director.
After a stronger writing field is removed, the overlap half-life grows from
0.177 to 5.247 rotational times, a factor of 30; under an incompatible new
pattern the rewrite half-time grows from 0.156 to 0.730, a factor of 4.7
(Supplemental Figs.~S6 and S7). Arbitrary two-dimensional easy-axis patterns
can be fabricated by digital photoalignment \cite{wu2012}, although the
particle--surface torque must be calibrated for a colloidal implementation.
This construction stores an externally specified code and provides a
device-oriented counterpart. The pair model separately tests memory encoded
autonomously by bond geometry.

The result identifies a pair-interaction route to hidden orientational
memory. Positional disorder prevents global director selection, while
capillary quadrupoles convert every bond direction into a local angular
compatibility condition. Four measurements expose the resulting state: the
memory-bearing population occupies a finite region of coupling space; global
order and independent-equilibrium overlap vanish with size; local pair order
survives; and descendants of one preparation remain correlated long after
spatial correlations have ended. The last separation is the central physical
effect. It suggests that distinct local angular histories can coexist without
a common optical axis.

Read as a design principle, this is a memory a material could develop rather
than be given. Nothing here is addressed, patterned, or written by an
external template: the code is the bond geometry the layer already has, the
carrier is a coordinate that stays free to move, and each preparation selects
its own compromise among the same fixed constraints. A layer therefore
records how it was made and driven while remaining, to the eye and to a
polarizer, unchanged. The disorder dependence sets the operating point for
building one, and it is not the naive limit: what stores most is the least
positional disorder that still hides the director, so an adaptive layer would
be tuned toward its ordering crossover rather than away from it. Whether a
layer can be driven to that point by its own environment, with interfacial
undulations both gating the barriers and adjusting the geometry that sets
them, is the open question this mechanism raises. Grooved substrates provide
a complementary test in which the spatial code is externally specified; in
the autonomous capillary model the code resides in the bond geometry itself.
