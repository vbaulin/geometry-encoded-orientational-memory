\section{Capillary origin of the bond-frame interaction}

At fixed particle geometry and interfacial contact, the interface height
\(u(\bm r)\) obeys the linearized Young--Laplace equation at scales below the
capillary length,
\begin{equation}
 \gamma\nabla^2 u=0.
 \label{eq:supp-young-laplace}
\end{equation}
For an isolated ellipsoid in this regime, the far-field solution is a harmonic
multipole expansion.
Averaging over force- and torque-free modes leaves the leading orientational mode
\begin{equation}
u_i(r,\phi)=\Delta u_{\max}\left(\frac{R}{r}\right)^2\cos2(\phi-\theta_i).
\end{equation}
with \((r,\phi)\) measured from the particle centre and \(\theta_i\) the
apolar axis.  This profile is the static equilibrium shape required to satisfy
anisotropic contact-angle constraints for an ellipsoidal contact line.

For two particles at separation \(r_{ij}\), with \(\phi_{ij}=\arg(\bm r_j-\bm r_i)\) and
\(\varphi_i=\theta_i-\phi_{ij}\), \(\varphi_j=\theta_j-\phi_{ij}\), interface additivity in
this far-field limit gives \(u=u_i+u_j\).  The capillary interaction is the cross
term in the
interface area cost,
\begin{equation}
 U_{ij}^{\rm cap}\approx
 -\gamma\int \nabla u_i\cdot\nabla u_j\,dA
 =-3\pi\gamma(\Delta u_{\max})^2
 \left(\frac{R}{r_{ij}}\right)^4
 \cos\left[2(\varphi_i+\varphi_j)\right],
 \label{eq:supp-capillary-relative}
\end{equation}
where \(\varphi_i,\varphi_j\) are the axes relative to the centre-to-centre
direction.  This is the second term in the
model's pair energy and can be written
\begin{equation}
U_{ij}^{\rm cap}\equiv -A_4(r_{ij})\cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij}),
\qquad
A_4(r_{ij})=3\pi\gamma(\Delta u_{\max})^2\left(\frac{R}{r_{ij}}\right)^4.
\end{equation}
For \(A_4>0\), each isolated bond is minimized by
\(\theta_i+\theta_j=2\phi_{ij}+m\pi\), where \(m\) is an integer.
Different bond angles on a disordered loop generally impose mutually
incompatible minima, so the many-particle ground state is a compromise among
bond-frame constraints rather than a uniform director.
Substitution of the two relative angles gives
\begin{equation}
 \cos(2\varphi_i+2\varphi_j)
 =\cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij}),
 \label{eq:supp-capillary-map}
\end{equation}
which is the second pair harmonic in the Letter.  This identity was tested
numerically to machine precision in
\texttt{tests/test\_rotating\_colloids\_capillary\_pair.py}.  Equation
\eqref{eq:supp-capillary-relative} is a far-field expression.  Near contact,
the interface shape and ellipsoidal geometry generate additional angular
harmonics.  The pairwise approximation is nevertheless accurate for a range
of experimentally studied configurations, and empirical pair potentials can
replace the quadrupolar expression when pair-calibration data are available
\cite{luo2019}.

The first term of the model,
\(-J_{ij}\cos2(\theta_i-\theta_j)\), is the leading apolar relative-angle
harmonic.  It represents short-range steric, near-field capillary, or other
shape-dependent alignment.  Keeping the two harmonics separate allows the
competition between relative alignment and bond-frame alignment to be
measured.  No one-body term of the form
\(-h_i\cos2(\theta_i-\alpha_i)\) is present after the temporary writing
field is removed.

\section{Caged positional graph}

The graph construction starts from an \(n\times n\) triangular monolayer with
nearest-neighbour spacing \(a=1\) and periodic boundaries.  Even values of
\(n\) close the staggered rows in a rectangular periodic cell.  Each centre
receives an independent Gaussian displacement with component standard
deviation \(0.16a\).  Edges connect all pairs within \(2.6a\).  Pair
displacements and bond angles are computed with the minimum-image
convention.  Rare pairs with \(r_{ij}<0.55a\) are rejected to prevent a
divergent \(r^{-4}\) weight.

For each graph, \(r_0\) is the median nearest-neighbour distance.  The
capillary and short-range weights are
\begin{align}
 w^+_{ij}&=\min\left[6,\left(\frac{r_0}{r_{ij}}\right)^4\right],
 \label{eq:supp-cap-weight}\\
 w^-_{ij}&=
 \begin{cases}
 \exp[-\max(r_{ij}/r_0-1,0)/0.2],&r_{ij}\leq1.35r_0,\\
 0,&r_{ij}>1.35r_0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:supp-short-weight}
\end{align}
The cap at 6 is a numerical guard against a rare near-overlap.  It is
inactive for every graph used in this work: across the 25 publication graphs
(\(N=144,256,576,1024,2304\) at seeds \(17,29,43,71,97\)) the largest
capillary weight is 3.87.  For \(N=1024\) at graph seed 17, the graph has
11741 edges, mean degree 22.93, capillary weighted degree 3.33, and
short-range weighted degree 1.60.  Its bare fourth bond-angle harmonic is
\(\left|\langle e^{4i\phi_{ij}}\rangle_E\right|=0.0011\), and the harmonic
stays within \(0.0007\)--\(0.0150\) over all 25 graphs.  The measured
\(G_2=0.447\pm0.011\) therefore arises from correlations between rotor angles
and bond frames, not from a pre-existing angular bias of the graph.

\section{Rotational dynamics}

All energies are in \(k_{\rm B}T\) and times in \(D_r^{-1}\).  The It\^{o}
equation integrated in the simulation is
\begin{equation}
 d\theta_i=
 -\partial_{\theta_i}\left(\frac{U}{k_{\rm B}T}\right)d(D_rt)
 +\sqrt{2\,d(D_rt)}\,dW_i,
 \label{eq:supp-langevin}
\end{equation}
where the \(dW_i\) are independent Wiener increments.  Euler--Maruyama
integration uses \(D_r\Delta t=0.0025\).  Angles are reduced modulo \(\pi\)
because the rotors are apolar.  The publication-scale state map uses 32
thermal replicas per graph, 20,000 burn-in steps, and 60,000 sampled steps,
with observables recorded every 100 steps.  The finite-size and matched
controls use 48 replicas, 30,000 burn-in steps, and 100,000 sampled steps at
the same recording interval.  The long-time protocols use 48 replicas, 24
split-replica parents, and extend to \(D_rt=625\) with observables recorded
every 200 steps.

\section{Order and memory observables}

The observables \(S,C_2,G_2\), and \(q_{\rm EA}\) are defined in the Letter.
Three additional quantities are used.

The overlap of independent replicas at a common final time is
\begin{equation}
 Q_{ab}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i e^{2i(\theta_i^{(a)}-\theta_i^{(b)})}.
 \label{eq:supp-replica}
\end{equation}
We plot \(|Q_{ab}|\).  Even independent random angles have a finite
Rayleigh floor
\(\langle|Q_{ab}|\rangle\simeq\sqrt{\pi}/(2\sqrt{N})\); this floor is shown
explicitly in Fig.~S1(c).

For split replicas, two copies start from the same equilibrated configuration
and then receive independent thermal noise.  Their real overlap is
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm split}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
 \cos2[\theta_i^{(a)}(t)-\theta_i^{(b)}(t)].
 \label{eq:supp-split}
\end{equation}
This protocol measures loss of basin identity without relying on a
time-averaged local mean.

The two-time autocorrelation following a random-state quench is
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm age}(t_w+\Delta t,t_w)=
 \frac{1}{N}\sum_i
 \left\langle\cos2[\theta_i(t_w+\Delta t)-\theta_i(t_w)]\right\rangle.
 \label{eq:supp-aging}
\end{equation}
Waiting-time dependence at a fixed lag is an aging diagnostic.  It does not,
by itself, establish a thermodynamic glass transition.

Each quenched graph carries its own offset in \(C_{\rm age}\), so the
waiting-time effect is resolved by the paired increment
\begin{equation}
 \Delta C_{\rm age}=
 C_{\rm age}(t_w^{\rm late}+\Delta t,t_w^{\rm late})-
 C_{\rm age}(t_w^{\rm early}+\Delta t,t_w^{\rm early}),
 \label{eq:supp-paired-aging}
\end{equation}
evaluated graph by graph before averaging.  At \(D_r\Delta t=30\),
\(D_rt_w^{\rm early}=62.5\) and \(D_rt_w^{\rm late}=375\), the three graphs
give \(+0.0262,+0.0232,+0.0266\), so
\(\Delta C_{\rm age}=+0.0253\pm0.0019\).  The unpaired between-graph standard
deviation of \(C_{\rm age}\) itself is 0.006, three times the increment, which
is why the paired form is reported in the Letter.

\section{Angular localization relative to a written target}

The write--release--read and split-replica overlaps measure a mean resultant
of the doubled angle, \(R=\langle\cos2\Delta\theta\rangle\), where
\(\Delta\theta\) is the per-particle angular error against the reference
state. Because only \(R\) is measured, the least-structured angular-error
distribution consistent with it is the maximum-entropy distribution. On the circle of the
doubled angle this is the von Mises density with concentration \(\kappa\)
fixed by
\begin{equation}
 \frac{I_1(\kappa)}{I_0(\kappa)}=R,
 \label{eq:supp-vonmises}
\end{equation}
where \(I_n\) is a modified Bessel function. Its differential entropy is
\(H=\ln[2\pi I_0(\kappa)]-\kappa I_1(\kappa)/I_0(\kappa)\), so the angular
localization relative to a uniform error is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal I=\ln 2\pi-H=
 \kappa\frac{I_1(\kappa)}{I_0(\kappa)}-\ln I_0(\kappa)
 \quad\text{nats.}
 \label{eq:supp-localization}
\end{equation}
Maximizing the entropy at fixed \(R\) minimizes this Kullback--Leibler
divergence. Equation~\eqref{eq:supp-localization} therefore bounds the
single-particle angular localization implied by the measured resultant. It is
not, without further assumptions, a mutual information or a memory capacity.
That identification would require a uniformly sampled target ensemble and a
shift-invariant additive-error channel. Summing particlewise values would
further require control of redundancy among the correlated rotors. The
present simulations use a fixed compatible target and archive a
particle-averaged overlap. We therefore report only the per-rotor localization
diagnostic and do not infer bits per layer.

\subsection{Basin channel and usable memory capacity}

The distinction between landscape multiplicity and usable memory can be
stated as a coarse-grained channel. Let \(W\) denote a written pattern,
\(B_0\) the basin entered after writing, \(B_t\) the basin occupied after a
field-free interval \(t\), and \(R\) the measured readout. Their conditional
distribution factors as
\begin{equation}
 p(r\mid w;t)=\sum_{b_0,b_t}p(r\mid b_t)
 K_t(b_t\mid b_0)p(b_0\mid w),
 \label{eq:supp-basin-channel}
\end{equation}
where \(p(b_0\mid w)\) measures write capture and \(K_t\) is the thermally
activated transition kernel between basins. The usable capacity at read time
\(t\) is
\begin{equation}
 C(t)=\max_{p(w)} I(W;R_t).
 \label{eq:supp-memory-capacity}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:supp-memory-capacity} separates the number of available
states from their addressability and survival. A count of one-flip-stable
states bounds the alphabet but is neither an entropy nor a device capacity
unless the states can be written and distinguished. The present fixed-target
protocol measures one diagonal retention channel. Estimating \(C(t)\) requires
an ensemble of prescribed targets and the complete post-release confusion
matrix.

\section{Finite-size state diagram and spatial correlations}

The state diagram in the Letter contains 441 coupling cells on a
\(21\times21\) grid.  Every cell is averaged over three independently
disordered \(N=400\) positional graphs.  For each graph, 32 thermal replicas
were equilibrated for 20,000 steps and sampled for 60,000 steps at
\(D_r\Delta t=0.0025\), with observables recorded every 100 steps.  The three
graph rows are averaged before classification.  Each resulting cell is
represented by
\begin{equation}
 \bm x=(S,C_2,G_2,q_{\rm EA},C_{\rm window}).
\end{equation}
The five coordinates are standardized across cells and clustered without
including \(J\) or \(g\).  K-means solutions with \(2\leq k\leq6\) give
silhouette scores \(0.384,0.417,0.451,0.444,\) and \(0.407\),
respectively; \(k=4\) is therefore selected.  Repeating the fit with 20
random initializations gives adjusted Rand index 1.0 relative to the
reported assignment.  Graph sensitivity was tested by exact enumeration of
all 27 ordered bootstrap resamples of the three graph rows.  Every resample
selected \(k=4\); the mean adjusted Rand index relative to the full
assignment was 0.972, with range 0.919--1.000.  Leaving out one graph at a
time gave adjusted Rand index at least 0.955.  The taxonomy is therefore
stable to the sampled positional graphs.  Its precise dimensional
decomposition remains observable-dependent: the silhouette difference
between \(k=4\) and \(k=5\) is 0.0075,
\(\operatorname{corr}(q_{\rm EA},C_{\rm window})=0.986\), and removing
\(C_2\) selects \(k=2\).  We consequently use the clusters to locate a
reproducible memory-bearing population, not to assign a thermodynamic phase
count. The persistence redundancy was tested directly. Removing
\(q_{\rm EA}\) or \(C_{\rm window}\) still selects \(k=4\), with adjusted
Rand indices 0.821 and 0.805 relative to the full assignment. The best-matched
hidden-memory population has Jaccard indices 0.918 and 0.906, respectively.
Replacing both persistence coordinates by their standardized mean also
selects \(k=4\), with adjusted Rand index 0.878 and hidden-population Jaccard
index 0.959. These ablations are recorded in
\path{capillary_regime_feature_ablation.json}. Regime names are assigned only after fitting, from
the observable centroids.  The confidence plotted in the Letter is
\begin{equation}
 m=\frac{d_2-d_1}{d_2},
\end{equation}
where \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) are the distances to the nearest and
second-nearest standardized centroids.  Small \(m\) marks cells for which
the finite-size taxonomy is unresolved.  Neither K-means boundaries nor
their names constitute thermodynamic phase boundaries.

\section{Write-release-read protocol}

A target configuration is first obtained from an independently equilibrated
copy of the autonomous pair model.  Independent replicas are then exposed
temporarily to the conjugate field
\begin{equation}
 U_{\rm write}/k_{\rm B}T=-h\sum_i
 \cos2(\theta_i-\theta_i^{\rm target}),
 \qquad h=1.5.
 \label{eq:supp-write}
\end{equation}
The field is removed completely before the release trajectory.  Readout uses
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\rm target}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
 \cos2[\theta_i(t)-\theta_i^{\rm target}].
 \label{eq:supp-target}
\end{equation}
At \(N=1024,J=4,g=5\), averaged over three quenched graphs, the overlap
reaches \(0.863\pm0.009\) while the field is applied and remains
\(0.441\pm0.021\) after a field-free interval of \(D_rt=625\).  The residual
global order is then \(S=0.065\pm0.008\) and the bond-frame order is
\(G_2=0.451\pm0.012\).  The corresponding split-replica overlap is
\(0.462\pm0.010\).  The field therefore selects a pre-existing
long-lived basin; it is not a permanent substrate term.  Repeating the same
write protocol with \(g=0\) gives an initial release overlap of
\(0.807\pm0.005\), but it falls below 0.2 by
\(D_r(t-t_{\rm off})=4.3\pm0.3\) after release and reaches
\(0.006\pm0.004\) at \(D_rt=625\).

The regular lattice demonstrates that high persistence alone does not imply

with increased random-bond frustration.  It is a useful upper-frustration
control, not an experimental capillary graph.

\section{Positional-disorder scan}

The couplings and the geometry are independent axes.  The state diagram
varies \(J\) and \(g\) at fixed disorder \(\sigma=0.16a\); here we fix
\((J,g)=(4,5)k_{\rm B}T\) and vary \(\sigma\) alone, with \(N=576\), five
quenched graphs per amplitude, 48 thermal replicas, 30,000 burn-in steps and
100,000 sampled steps at \(D_r\Delta t=0.0025\).  The graph construction
rejects \(\sigma>0.28a\), because independent Gaussian displacements of that
size begin to produce particle overlaps; \(0.28a\) is therefore the end of
the accessible range and not a chosen endpoint.

Two features distinguish this from a simple loss of order.  First, the two
quantities separate: over the scanned range \(S\) falls by a factor of 13.4
while \(q_{\rm EA}\) falls by 1.34, so the disorder removes the director
without removing the persistence.  Second, \(\mathcal H\) increases
monotonically and its increments decelerate, 0.136, 0.120, 0.109, 0.055 and
0.032 for the successive steps above \(\sigma=0.05a\); a geometric
extrapolation of the last two places the asymptote near 0.53, so the scan
ends within roughly ten percent of a plateau.  There is no interior maximum,
and the operating point \(\sigma=0.16a\) used elsewhere in this work is
sufficient rather than optimal.

The small-\(\sigma\) limit is informative about the mechanism.  At
\(\sigma=0.02a\) the bond-frame order is \(G_2=0.014\), that is, essentially
unsatisfied, while \(C_2=0.927\) and \(S=0.916\): the near-regular graph
cannot satisfy \(\theta_i+\theta_j=2\phi_{ij}+m\pi\) on its three
inequivalent bond directions simultaneously, so the system abandons those
conditions and follows the alignment harmonic into a conventional nematic.
The bare fourth bond-angle harmonic remains below 0.006 at every amplitude
and in fact grows with \(\sigma\), which excludes the alternative reading
that the ordered state at small \(\sigma\) reflects a residual angular bias
of the edge distribution.  Positional disorder is what makes local subsets of
the bond-frame conditions mutually satisfiable, and the memory appears with
them.

To distinguish finite-window persistence from equilibrium replica ordering,
we performed a separate 200-point scan along
\begin{equation}
 (J,g)=\lambda(4,5)k_{\rm B}T,
 \qquad
 \lambda\in\{0.30,0.45,0.60,0.75,0.90,1.05,1.20,1.40\}.
 \label{eq:supp-ray}
\end{equation}
The scan used \(N=144,256,576,1024,2304\), five independent quenched graphs
at every point, and 48 independently initialized thermal replicas per graph.
Each replica was equilibrated for 50,000 steps and sampled for 100,000 steps.

For each replica pair define the local complex overlap
\(q_i^{ab}=\exp[2i(\theta_i^a-\theta_i^b)]\) and
\(Q_{ab}=N^{-1}\sum_iq_i^{ab}\).  The zero-wave-vector overlap
susceptibility is
\begin{equation}
 \chi_Q=N\langle|Q_{ab}|^2\rangle.
 \label{eq:supp-chiq}
\end{equation}
Writing \(\chi_Q(\bm k)=N\langle|N^{-1}\sum_iq_i^{ab}
e^{i\bm k\cdot\bm r_i}|^2\rangle\), the directional second-moment lengths are
\begin{equation}
 \xi_\mu=\frac{1}{2\sin(k_{\min,\mu}/2)}
 \left[\frac{\chi_Q(0)}{\chi_Q(k_{\min,\mu})}-1\right]^{1/2},
 \qquad \mu=x,y,
 \label{eq:supp-xi}
\end{equation}
and we report \(\xi_L=(\xi_x\xi_y)^{1/2}\) divided by the geometric-mean box
length.  A finite-temperature glass transition would normally produce a
size-independent crossing of \(\xi_L/L\), together with nonvanishing
inter-replica overlap or a growing \(\chi_Q\).

No such signature is present.  At \(\lambda=0.30,0.75,1.40\), log--log fits
give
\begin{equation}
 \langle|Q_{ab}|\rangle\propto
 N^{-0.490},\quad N^{-0.472},\quad N^{-0.485},
 \label{eq:supp-overlap-exponents}
\end{equation}
respectively.  The corresponding largest-size susceptibilities are 1.74,
7.10, and 7.78; they remain finite as \(N\) grows.  The \(\xi_L/L\) curves
decrease with size and do not share a crossing over the scanned interval.
The unnormalized length is itself short.  Over all 40 coupling--size cells,
the graph-averaged \(\xi_L\) lies between \(0.15a\) and \(1.49a\), and no
single graph exceeds \(2.78a\).  The overlap correlation length therefore
agrees with the independent real-space estimate \(r/a\simeq1.73\) obtained
from \(C_2(r)\) and \(G_2(r)\), although the two are measured from different
observables on different runs.
Thus the large finite-window \(q_{\rm EA}\) measures slow trajectory memory,
not an equilibrium Edwards--Anderson order parameter.  The distinction is
important: the write--release and split-replica protocols demonstrate
retention, while independent replicas demonstrate the absence of a common
frozen state.

\section{Programmable mosaic easy-axis realization}

The autonomous model in the Letter stores its local reference frames in the
centre-to-centre bonds.  We also simulated a programmable construction in
which a surface pattern supplies prescribed easy axes \(\alpha_i\).  In this
section only, the dimensionless angular potential is
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{\rm gr}=-j\sum_{(ij)}w_{ij}\cos2(\theta_i-\theta_j)
 -h\sum_i\cos2(\theta_i-\alpha_i),
 \label{eq:supp-groove-potential}
\end{equation}
and the dynamics obey
\begin{equation}
 \partial_\tau P=\sum_i\partial_{\theta_i}
 \left(\partial_{\theta_i}P+P\partial_{\theta_i}\Phi_{\rm gr}\right),
 \qquad \tau=D_rt.
 \label{eq:supp-groove-smol}
\end{equation}
Here \(j\) and \(h\) are deterministic angular-relaxation rates normalized by
rotational diffusion.  The first term transmits orientation
between neighbours; the second registers each rotor to the local patterned
axis.  Its direct readout is
\begin{equation}
 G_2^{\alpha}=N^{-1}\sum_i\cos2(\theta_i-\alpha_i).
 \label{eq:supp-groove-registration}
\end{equation}

The archived construction is deliberately segmented.  The static scan uses
a \(32\times32\) array divided into sixteen \(8\times8\) patches; the dynamic
protocol uses a \(16\times16\) array with four patches.  Patch axes advance by
\(\pi/4\), so their laboratory-frame nematic contributions cancel.  Within a
patch the interaction graph is dense, whereas adjacent patches are joined by
two links of weight 0.18.  The results therefore test a patterned
easy-axis device with weak interpatch coupling, not a homogeneous grooved
monolayer.

The static \(29\times29\) scan contains 841 parameter cells and 48 thermal
replicas per cell.  The operational conditions
\(S\leq0.35\), \(C_2\geq0.70\), \(G_2^{\alpha}\geq0.70\), and
\(q_{\rm EA}\geq0.50\) select 824 cells in one connected grid component.  A
stricter cut, \(S\leq0.05\), \(C_2,G_2^{\alpha}\geq0.80\), and
\(q_{\rm EA}\geq0.70\), retains 688 cells.  These counts describe correlated
grid cells, not independent samples or a thermodynamic phase.  They establish
that low global order and strong pattern registration occupy an extended
parameter region of this finite device model.

Static persistence is not an independent memory test here.  Across the
mosaic scan, \(q_{\rm EA}\) and \((G_2^{\alpha})^2\) have correlation
0.999987 and root-mean-square difference 0.00178.  This near identity is
expected when each stationary angular distribution is symmetric around its
prescribed axis.  It shows that the field-on statistic records the imposed
pattern.  Removal and rewriting protocols are needed to measure retention.

\section{Separate weak-link mosaic control}

An earlier groove-free mosaic construction rotated locally square domains and joined
them with a fixed number of weak crosslinks.  It produced nearly simultaneous
large \(C_2\) and \(G_2\), but only 0.6228\% of its total coupling weight
crossed domain boundaries.  In the strong-locking limit its domain directors
reduce exactly to an Ising Hamiltonian
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rm eff}/J=-\sum_{bc}K_{bc}s_bs_c,\qquad s_b=\pm1.
\end{equation}
Exact enumeration found 16--78 single-domain-flip-stable states across 20
graph jitters, confirming a multibasin mechanism.  The same calculation also
showed that the ground-state unsatisfied weight was only 0.75\% of the total
crosslink weight.  The mosaic model is therefore retained as a mechanism
test, not as evidence for the physical capillary regime.  The Letter instead
uses a caged disordered monolayer with no imposed orientational domains.

\section{Loop incompatibility and basin multiplicity}

The strong-locking reduction permits a direct intervention on the source of
the multibasin structure. Write
\begin{equation}
 H_{\rm eff}=-\sum_{(bc)}K_{bc}s_bs_c,
 \qquad
 \mathcal W_\gamma=\prod_{(bc)\in\gamma}{\rm sgn}(K_{bc}),
 \label{eq:supp-loop-holonomy}
\end{equation}
where \(\gamma\) is a closed path in the domain graph. Changing the sign used
to name a local director, \(s_b\to\eta_bs_b\) with \(\eta_b=\pm1\), sends
\(K_{bc}\to\eta_b\eta_cK_{bc}\) but leaves
\(\mathcal W_\gamma\) unchanged. Thus \(\mathcal W_\gamma=-1\) belongs to
the loop rather than to a chosen set of local axes. At least one bond on such
a loop must depart from its preferred relation. This loop product is the
discrete curvature of the coupling network: local angular relations fail to
close after transport around the path.

For each quenched graph we compared the measured coupling network with the
minimum-weight globally flat sign network. The two networks have the same
vertices, edges, and \(|K_{bc}|\) on every edge; only the loop products differ.
A second control applies a local sign relabelling to the original network and
therefore preserves every loop product. Exact enumeration confirms that this
gauge-equivalent control has the same energies and stable-state count to
machine precision.

Across 24 independently jittered graphs, removing nontrivial loop products
reduced the number of one-flip-stable states by \(5.58\) on average. The
graph-bootstrap 95\% interval for the original-minus-flat difference is
\(2.67\)--\(8.83\). The effect is on the number of locally stable angular
compromises. It is not a uniform increase in their lifetimes:
finite-temperature write--release dynamics gave an original-minus-flat
overlap-area difference of \(-3.48\times10^{-6}\), with interval
\([-1.56\times10^{-4},1.47\times10^{-4}]\).

We repeated the intervention in the continuous-angle pair Hamiltonian by
rotating only the preferred phases of cross-domain bonds. The graph, bond
amplitudes, positional disorder, thermal-noise histories, and induced
\(|K_{bc}|\) were matched; the maximum relative error in the coupling
magnitudes was \(1.24\times10^{-15}\). Transforming the Hamiltonian, angular
state, and written target by the same local gauge changed the complete
dynamical trajectory by at most \(6.89\times10^{-14}\).

The mean original-minus-flat retention area over four prescribed written
patterns was \(0.00286\), with a graph-bootstrap interval
\([-0.00011,0.00570]\). The target-resolved response reveals what this average
conceals: the uniform director changes by \(-0.00878\), with interval
\([-0.01202,-0.00519]\), whereas a fixed random-domain pattern changes by
\(+0.01084\), with interval \([0.00211,0.01938]\). Loop incompatibility
redistributes stability among angular basins.

We then separated writing from release by preparing the original and
loop-flattened networks in identical angular configurations, removing the
write field, and applying paired Brownian increments.  The scan contained
eight graphs, fourteen prescribed targets per graph, and 48 descendants per
target, with \(g/J=1.614\) and an observation time \(D_rt=100\).  Let
\(\Delta R\) denote the original-minus-flat difference in the time-integrated,
initially normalized target overlap.  At intermediate coupling the flattened
network retained more: \(\Delta R=-0.02599\) at \(\beta J=0.6\), with a
graph-bootstrap interval \([-0.03175,-0.02050]\), and
\(\Delta R=-0.02579\) at \(\beta J=0.8\), with interval
\([-0.03052,-0.02119]\).  The difference weakened as both landscapes became
more strongly confining.  From \(\beta J=1.2\) to 2.4 every interval included
zero; no coupling showed a resolved retention advantage for the incompatible
network.  Thus the apparent change of sign in the mean response is not a
resolved crossover.  The physical result is instead a nonmonotonic response
to loop flattening: the difference is small at \(\beta J=0.4\), largest at
\(\beta J=0.6\)--0.8, and unresolved from \(\beta J=1.2\) onward.  The
absolute survival areas identify the two limits.  They rise from 0.0153 and
0.0174 for the original and flattened networks at \(\beta J=0.4\) to 0.6861
and 0.6856 at \(\beta J=2.4\).  Weak coupling therefore permits Brownian
erasure in both networks, whereas strong coupling traps the preparation in
both on the observation window.  Loop flattening improves survival only in
the intermediate escape window; the incompatible network never acquires a
resolved lifetime advantage.

Across every coupling, the target-resolved retention difference decreased as
the energetic advantage of the flattened target increased.  Ordinary basin
energetics therefore explains part of the redistribution.  Because flattening
also changes the target torque and local Hessian, this comparison does not
assign the release difference to cycle holonomy alone.  It establishes the
sharper separation used in the Letter: incompatible loops enlarge the
catalogue of available compromises, whereas the survival of a selected
compromise is set jointly by its energy, local restoring torques, and escape
barriers.

These calculations separate two parts of the memory mechanism. Global
incompatibility supplies a larger set of metastable states. Writing chooses
one state from that set, and the energy barriers determine how long thermal
motion remains in it. The loop structure therefore controls basin multiplicity
in this reduction, while retention is a joint property of the chosen pattern
and its barriers. The phase intervention acts directly on the angular
Hamiltonian. At an unpatterned interface the preferred phase is the
line-of-centres angle, so an experimental version of this matched comparison
requires patterned bond anisotropy or a geometrically embedded flat control.

\subsection{Particle-resolved localization of retained memory}

The same trajectories can determine where the retained state resides. For
two descendants of one preparation, define the local overlap
\begin{equation}
 m_i(t)=\left\langle
 \cos 2[\theta_i^{(1)}(t)-\theta_i^{(2)}(t)]
 \right\rangle .
 \label{eq:supp-local-memory}
\end{equation}
For every cycle \(C\) based at particle \(i\), let \(\mathcal T_C\) denote the
composed bond-frame transport. A local common-fixed-point residual is
\begin{equation}
 d_i=\min_{\theta}\sum_{C\ni i}w_C
 d_\pi^2[\mathcal T_C(\theta),\theta],
 \label{eq:supp-local-incompatibility}
\end{equation}
where \(d_\pi\) is angular distance modulo \(\pi\) and \(w_C\) fixes the cycle
weighting convention. This residual vanishes when the incident cycle maps
share a fixed apolar orientation. The connected spatial correlation
\begin{equation}
 C_{md}(r)=\left\langle
 [m_i-\bar m][d_j-\bar d]
 \right\rangle_{r_{ij}\simeq r}
 \label{eq:supp-memory-defect-correlation}
\end{equation}
distinguishes three locations for memory. A positive short-range value places
retention on incompatible cores; a finite-distance maximum places it on their
boundaries; a negative short-range value places it in compatible patches.
Degree, local bond energy, and initial torque must be conditioned when these
correlations are compared across disordered graphs.

\section{Experimental parameter estimate}

The required separation between positional and rotational motion has an
experimental precedent.  Charged silica rods have been observed in a state
with strongly caged translational coordinates and nearly free rotations
\cite{besseling2026}; DNA-based sliding contacts provide a second method for
constructing thermally driven colloidal pivots \cite{melio2026}.  Neither
experiment implements the capillary memory interaction, but both establish
that a persistent positional scaffold can coexist with Brownian angular
motion.

The static-torque assumption also requires the interfacial
deformation to remain quasistatic over the rotational time scale.
For a meniscus mode of lateral extent \(\ell\), a conservative viscous estimate is
\[
 \tau_{\rm int} \sim \frac{\eta_{\rm eff}\,\ell}{\gamma},
\qquad
 \tau_{\rm int}D_r \ll 1,
\]
with \(\eta_{\rm eff}\) the effective (sum) interfacial viscosity and \(\gamma\) the
interfacial tension.  With \(\eta_{\rm eff}=1\)--\(5\,\mathrm{mPa\,s}\),
\(\gamma=10\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}\), and \(\ell\sim 2R=4\)--\(20\,\mu\mathrm m\),
this gives \(\tau_{\rm int}\sim10^{-7}\)--\(10^{-5}\,\mathrm s\), so the condition is
safely met when \(D_r^{-1}\) is at least \(10^{-2}\,\mathrm s\) and is typically
much longer in the reported caged interfacial protocol.
The same check is implemented by
\texttt{scripts/estimate\_capillary\_timescale.py}.

For the interfacial realization, the pair torque should be measured before a
dense layer is assembled.  Two ellipsoids held at separation \(r\) give
\begin{equation}
 \frac{U_{\rm eff}(\theta_i,\theta_j\mid r)}{k_{\rm B}T}
 =-\ln p(\theta_i,\theta_j\mid r)+\mathrm{const.}
 \label{eq:supp-pair-calibration}
\end{equation}
from their stationary angular histogram.  Projecting
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-pair-calibration} onto
\(\cos2(\theta_i-\theta_j)\) and
\(\cos2(\theta_i+\theta_j-2\phi_{ij})\) determines the two coefficients in
the model.  Repeating the measurement at several separations tests the
\(r^{-4}\) capillary amplitude independently of collective memory.

Solving Eq.~(1) of the Letter for the meniscus amplitude gives
\begin{equation}
 \Delta u_{\max}=
 \left[
 \frac{A_4}{3\pi\gamma}
 \left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^4
 \right]^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:supp-meniscus}
\end{equation}
At room temperature, take \(\gamma=10\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}\) and \(r/R=3\).
The corresponding energy--amplitude pairs are
\begin{equation}
 \left(\frac{A_4}{k_{\rm B}T},\frac{\Delta u_{\max}}{\mathrm{nm}}\right)
 =(1,1.88),\ (3,3.26),\ (5,4.20).
\end{equation}
Smooth
micron-scale ellipsoids often generate much larger deformations and
interactions far above \(k_{\rm B}T\) \cite{loudet2005}.  Surface
nanostructure can reduce the attraction by an order of magnitude and allow
electrostatic repulsion to maintain finite particle separations
\cite{trevenen2023}.  However, strongly porous ellipsoids lose the
quadrupolar meniscus.  A suitable experiment should therefore tune roughness,
wetting, charge, salt concentration, and surface coverage jointly, and verify
the pair torque before attempting the many-particle memory protocol.

\section{Curvature writing and wave gating}

A deformation of the host interface couples directly to the
particle-induced quadrupole.  In the small-slope far field, the orienting
energy can be written
\begin{equation}
 U_i^{\rm curv}=-\pi\gamma H_pR_p^2\Delta c(\bm r_i,t)
 \cos2(\theta_i-\alpha_i),
 \label{eq:supp-curvature-write}
\end{equation}
where \(\Delta c\) is the local deviatoric curvature, \(\alpha_i\) is its
principal axis, \(H_p\) is the particle-induced quadrupole amplitude, and
\(R_p\) is its lateral scale.  This coupling has been measured through
curvature-driven orientation and migration of interfacial rods
\cite{cavallaro2011}.  It provides a native write protocol: apply a shaped
curvature field, allow the angular state to relax, flatten the interface, and
measure \(Q_{\rm target}\) only after Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-curvature-write}
has vanished.

For \(\gamma=10\,\mathrm{mN\,m^{-1}}\), \(H_p=5\,\mathrm{nm}\), and
\(R_p=1\,\mu\mathrm m\), a \(k_{\rm B}T\) orienting scale requires
\(\Delta c\simeq26\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\).  If the imposed interface profile is
\(h=A\cos kx\), this curvature corresponds to
\(A\simeq6.6\,\mathrm{nm}\) at wavelength \(100\,\mu\mathrm m\), or
\(A\simeq0.66\,\mu\mathrm m\) at \(1\,\mathrm{mm}\).  These values are
order-of-magnitude targets because the numerical prefactor depends on the
wetting geometry and curvature convention.

A spatially uniform oscillation does not choose \(\alpha_i\), but it can
modulate the pair barriers.  With
\(\Delta u(t)=\Delta u_0[1+\epsilon\cos\omega t]\) and
\(g\propto\gamma(\Delta u)^2\),
\begin{equation}
 \frac{g(t)}{g_0}\simeq
 1+2\epsilon\cos\omega t+
 \frac{\epsilon^2}{2}\left(1+\cos2\omega t\right).
 \label{eq:supp-wave-gate}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:supp-wave-gate} predicts an interaction gate: weak forcing
anneals local angular basins, whereas a trough that crosses the onset seen in
Fig.~4 of the Letter accelerates erasure.

A wave strong enough to displace the particles acts on a second variable.
Table~\ref{tab:supp-disorder-retention} measures retention against the
positional disorder \(\sigma\), and the dependence is not monotonic: the
connected overlap peaks near \(\sigma\simeq0.11a\), falls by 31\% at
\(0.28a\), and collapses below \(0.11a\) because the director is no longer
suppressed.  If an undulation changes \(\sigma\) slowly compared with
\(D_r^{-1}\), the layer moves quasistatically along that curve, and the sign
of the effect is fixed by which side of the peak it starts from.  A layer
prepared at \(\sigma>0.11a\) loses retention when the wave loosens the cage
further and gains it when the wave anneals the positions toward the peak; a
layer prepared below the peak is erased by any excursion that lets the
director reform.  This makes wave forcing a signed operation rather than a
generic disturbance, and it is measurable with the same trajectories: the
positional record gives \(\sigma(t)\) and the angular record gives the
retention, so the predicted curve can be traversed within one experiment.  It
also suggests, without demonstrating, a route to a self-tuning layer, since
the operating point that maximizes retention lies at the ordering crossover
rather than at extreme disorder.  Low-frequency capillary waves
have annealed air--water colloidal monolayers \cite{caso2025}, and stronger
ultrasonic forcing produces dynamic capillary interactions together with
hydrodynamic rearrangement \cite{huerre2018}.  A memory experiment must
therefore record both \(\theta_i(t)\) and \(\bm r_i(t)\).  The useful
separation of scales is
\(\tau_{\rm int}\ll\omega^{-1}\sim D_r^{-1}\ll\tau_{\rm cage}\): the
interface follows the drive, orientations respond within a cycle, and the
constraint graph remains fixed.

The many-particle experiment then has a direct rejection sequence.  First,
verify \(\tau_{\rm cage}D_r\gg1\) from position and orientation trajectories.
Second, measure \(S,C_2,G_2\) without a writing field.  Third, split repeated
preparations by applying independent thermal histories, or write a target
with a temporary polarized optical field and read only after removal.  The
claim is rejected if low \(S\) is accompanied by vanishing \(G_2\), if the
field-free overlap is indistinguishable from the low-quadrupole control, or
if retention disappears when the observation window is extended.

\section{Order of operations as a stored variable}

The single-pattern protocol establishes retention of a prepared angular
state.  A stronger test asks whether two histories containing the same
operations can be distinguished after both controls have been removed.  We
represent the two temporary fields by
\begin{equation}
 \frac{U_X}{k_{\rm B}T}=-h\sum_i m_i^X
 \cos2(\theta_i-\alpha_i^X),\qquad X\in\{A,B\},
 \label{eq:supp-order-fields}
\end{equation}
where \(m_i^X\) is a binary support mask.  Pattern \(A\) is an equilibrated
configuration of the autonomous pair model, and
\(\alpha_i^B=\alpha_i^A+\pi/2\).  Each pulse lasts \(D_rt=5\).  We apply
\(A\) then \(B\), or \(B\) then \(A\), set \(h=0\), and follow both states
for a further \(D_rt=20\).  The histories start from the same configurations
and use common Brownian increments at every corresponding time step, so their
difference is caused by pulse order rather than independent thermal noise.

For either history \(X\), define the overlap with pattern \(A\) on the read
support \({\cal S}\) as
\begin{equation}
 Q_A^X(t)=\frac{1}{|{\cal S}|}\sum_{i\in{\cal S}}
 \cos2[\theta_i^X(t)-\alpha_i^A].
\end{equation}
The signed order readout is
\begin{equation}
 M_{\rm ord}(t)=\frac{Q_A^{BA}(t)-Q_A^{AB}(t)}{2}.
 \label{eq:supp-order-readout}
\end{equation}
A positive value identifies which opposed pattern acted last.  This readout
must be distinguished from the unsigned separation
\(1-|{\cal S}|^{-1}\sum_{i\in{\cal S}}
\cos2(\theta_i^{AB}-\theta_i^{BA})\).  Nonlinear Brownian trajectories can
separate while carrying no decodable information about pulse order; only
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-order-readout} tests the order bit.

Two support geometries isolate the mechanism.  In the contested protocol,
both fields act on the same quarter-width stripe and favor orthogonal
orientations there.  In the partitioned control, the fields act on two
quarter-width stripes separated by one quarter of the periodic box.  The
two partitioned masks share no particle, and no interaction edge directly
joins them in any of the tested graphs.  The autonomous pair Hamiltonian is
unchanged between protocols.

The \(N=144\) scan uses three independently disordered graphs and 12 thermal
replicas per graph.  At \(h=k_{\rm B}T\), both protocols give a terminal
readout consistent with zero.  The contested response appears on the sampled
grid between \(h=2\) and \(3k_{\rm B}T\), reaching
\(M_{\rm ord}=0.355\pm0.078\) at \(h=3k_{\rm B}T\),
\(0.586\pm0.010\) at \(5k_{\rm B}T\), and
\(0.5985\pm0.0015\) at \(8k_{\rm B}T\).  The corresponding partitioned
values are \(0.007\pm0.025\), \(0.022\pm0.002\), and
\(0.0265\pm0.0104\).  Errors are standard errors over quenched graphs.  Over
the final quarter of the \(h=8k_{\rm B}T\) release trajectory, the contested
slope is \((0.3\pm3.5)\times10^{-3}\) per rotational time and is unresolved
from zero.  Repeating the endpoint at \(N=256\) gives
\(0.5781\pm0.0030\) for contested writes and
\(0.0015\pm0.0010\) for partitioned writes.
A separate \(N=144\) support test applies both fields to all particles at
\(h=8k_{\rm B}T\).  Its terminal readout is
\(M_{\rm ord}=0.667\pm0.022\), compared with
\(0.5985\pm0.0015\) for the quarter-width support.  The sequence record is
therefore not produced by the edge of the driven stripe, and it does not
require an undriven reference region in the capillary-rotor model.

Common Brownian increments isolate the order-dependent driven response, but
they suppress ordinary replica decorrelation. We therefore repeated the
sequence test with independent noise for every \(AB\) and \(BA\) history.
Five independently disordered \(N=256\) graphs were used, with 48 replicas
for each pulse order and graph. Table~\ref{tab:supp-independent-order}
reports means and standard errors over graphs. The contested protocol becomes
decodable between \(h=1\) and \(2k_{\rm B}T\), reaches 98.96\% accuracy at
\(3k_{\rm B}T\), and reaches 100\% at \(5\) and \(8k_{\rm B}T\). At the
largest field, its terminal readout is \(0.6336\pm0.0198\), compared with
\(0.0123\pm0.0027\) for partitioned writes. The difference between the two
graph-averaged readouts is \(0.6213\pm0.0200\). Partitioned writes retain a
weak residual bias at the

not approach the contested separation. Pulse order is therefore readable
from individual noisy trajectories and is not an artifact of common-random-
number cancellation.

The general criterion can be stated without reference to a particular
substrate.  Let \(\Phi_A\) and \(\Phi_B\) denote the finite-time write maps,
\({\cal R}_t\) field-free relaxation, and \({\cal B}\) the basin readout.
Define \(\Pi_t={\cal B}{\cal R}_t\), and call two post-write states equivalent
when \(\Pi_t(\bm x)=\Pi_t(\bm y)\).  The quotient
\({\cal X}/{\sim_t}\) is then the set of records that remain distinguishable
after release.  Order is retained only if
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_t\Phi_B\Phi_A(\bm\theta_0)\ne
 \Pi_t\Phi_A\Phi_B(\bm\theta_0).
 \label{eq:supp-order-map}
\end{equation}
The exchange \(A\leftrightarrow B\) is broken in the retained state when this
inequality holds.  The signed variable \(M_{\rm ord}\) is its order parameter.
A nonzero Lie bracket of the driven vector fields, or a finite difference
between \(\Phi_B\Phi_A\bm\theta_0\) and
\(\Phi_A\Phi_B\bm\theta_0\) immediately after writing, establishes
order-sensitive dynamics but not memory: \(\Pi_t\) can still map the two
states to the same equivalence class.

The failure of bracket magnitude to predict retention is explicit in an
eight-coordinate multibasin reduction,
\begin{equation}
 \dot q_i=-q_i(q_i^2-q_{\rm w}^2)
 -c\sum_j L_{ij}q_j+f_i^X ,
 \label{eq:supp-double-well-order}
\end{equation}
where \(q_i=\pm q_{\rm w}\) label two local wells, \(L\) is the ring-graph
Laplacian, \(c\) is the coupling, and \(f_i^X\) is the temporary force from
operation \(X\).  The two operations exert opposite forces on \(k\) common
coordinates.  At \(q_{\rm w}=0.65\), fixed drive amplitude 12, and
\(c=0.30\), neither extreme retains order: \(k=0\) and \(k=8\) both return to
the same basin after release.  By contrast, \(k=4,5,6\) place all eight
coordinates in different wells under the two pulse orders.  For \(k=4\),
four retained coordinates were never directly driven, showing that coupling
transmits the sequence distinction beyond the write support.

The full-support null is not a weak-response limit.  The Lie-bracket norm is
11.70 at \(k=8\), larger than 8.89 at \(k=4\), and the maximum AB/BA
separation immediately after writing is nearly identical
(1.6958 and 1.6965).  Release erases the first difference and retains the
second at 1.30.  At weaker coupling \(c=0.13\), full support again retains
four coordinates, so the re-entrant null results from collective-mode
selection rather than support geometry alone.

For binary write masks, the spatial overlap variable
\(s_i=m_i^Am_i^B\) has contrast
\begin{equation}
 \chi_{\rm ov}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i(s_i-\bar s)^2 .
\end{equation}
It vanishes for disjoint supports (\(s_i=0\)) and uniform full contest
(\(s_i=1\)), and is positive for partial contest.  In the double-well
reduction, \(\chi_{\rm ov}\) is the control that separates the retained
exchange-broken regime from the two exchange-symmetric limits.  The rotor
result at full support shows that this particular re-entrant structure is not
universal.  What transfers between the two models is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:supp-order-map}: microscopic noncommutation must survive the
relaxation map as a difference between basin labels.

Equation~\eqref{eq:supp-order-map} is a criterion for sequence storage in a
relaxing material.  It assumes dissipation and a specified readout and
therefore does not define a microscopic arrow of time.

\section{Submission validation results}

Three tests address assumptions that are not resolved by the fixed-graph
production runs. They are launched together by
\path{scripts/run_rotating_colloids_prl_submission_validations_4gpu.sh}. Each
job writes append-only JSONL records and resumes from completed graph--parameter
keys.

The time-step test held every physical duration fixed while reducing
\(D_r\Delta t\) from 0.0025 to 0.00125 and 0.000625. It compares
\(S,C_2,G_2,q_{\rm EA},C_{\rm window}\), the split-replica endpoint, and the
written-state endpoint on five independently disordered \(N=576\) graphs.
Relative to the finest step, the production step changes \(C_2,G_2,q_{\rm
EA}\), and \(C_{\rm window}\) by at most 0.0031. Its paired split-endpoint
difference is \(0.0114\pm0.0149\). The written endpoint changes by
\(-0.0396\pm0.0150\), whereas the intermediate step differs from the finest
by \(-0.0105\pm0.0216\). Thus the state observables and split memory are
weakly converged at the production step. The written endpoint has a resolved
coarse-step bias, which underestimates rather than creates the retained
signal.

The mobile-centre test replaces the fixed graph by overdamped translational
motion. Capillary and alignment forces act together with a soft excluded-volume
core and an isotropic cage potential
\begin{equation}
 U_{\rm cage}=\frac{k_{\rm cage}}{2}
 \sum_i|\bm r_i-\bm r_i^0|^2.
\end{equation}
The dimensionless translational mobility is
\(D_t/(a^2D_r)\). The scan reports split-replica retention, root-mean-square
centre displacement, minimum separation, force-clipping frequency, and the
fraction of initial graph edges that survive. A tested cage stiffness passes
only if the endpoint overlap exceeds 0.30, root-mean-square displacement is
below \(0.10a\), at least 95\% of initial edges remain, and fewer than 1\% of
positional force evaluations are clipped. Passing defines a finite-time
operating window for the effective mobile model, not an equilibrium glass
state. The first passing stiffness is
\(k_{\rm cage}=500k_{\rm B}T/a^2\), where
\(Q_{\rm split}=0.466\pm0.032\), the root-mean-square displacement is
\((0.0917\pm0.0043)a\), and \(96.33\pm0.19\%\) of initial edges survive.
At \(k_{\rm cage}=2000k_{\rm B}T/a^2\), these values become
\(0.577\pm0.029\), \((0.0311\pm0.0006)a\), and
\(98.56\pm0.06\%\). The quadrupolar interaction is retained in this
mobile-centre calculation. Because the soft repulsion permits nominal-core
overlap, the calculation tests robustness to centre motion and neighbour
changes but not hard-particle excluded volume.

The sequence test separates two uses of thermal noise. Common Brownian
increments isolate the deterministic difference between \(AB\) and \(BA\).
Independent increments test experimental readability. For the latter, each
history is generated independently and classified by the sign of its overlap
with pattern \(A\). The report gives balanced single-trajectory decoding
accuracy and \(d'\) across five \(N=256\) graphs. The field-resolved results
in Table~\ref{tab:supp-independent-order} establish single-trajectory
readability for contested writes under independent Brownian histories.

\section{Discovery and validation sequence}

The calculations were organized so that the same statistic did not both
define and validate the result.  The exploratory stage was the 441-cell
observable-space map.  Coupling coordinates and physical names were excluded
from clustering.  The selected point \((J,g)=(4,5)k_{\rm B}T\) was then fixed
before the larger-size controls and long trajectories were analysed.  The
confirmatory stages were: five-graph selected-point replication, removal of
the capillary channel, regular and shuffled-frame controls, split-replica and
write--release dynamics, and the independent 200-point finite-size overlap
scan.  The programmable groove calculations use a different Hamiltonian and
are not pooled with these tests; they show how the same readout logic can be
implemented with an externally specified angular code.
